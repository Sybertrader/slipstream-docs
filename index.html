The problem

Release dates slip in the gaps between teams.

01

The weekly chase

Release managers, TPMs and engineering directors spend hours in spreadsheets and status meetings trying to answer one question: will this date hold?

02

The missing link

With several squads on one shared release, the blocker usually sits in another team’s project. Often it’s only an issue key in a comment, and nobody drew the link.

03

The expensive workaround

Jira Plans comes only with Jira Premium and Enterprise, which you buy for the whole site. And it plans from the dependencies people remembered to link.

What Slipstream answers

Three answers before the status meeting, from the Jira work you already have.

The blocker nobody linked

Slipstream keeps the Blocks links your teams created and adds implicit ones it finds in Jira text. Every edge shows how sure it is. Slipstream doesn’t connect to your repositories: a branch or PR name counts only if someone wrote it into Jira.

Whether the date still holds

Every Fix Version with a release date gets a PERT confidence and a projected finish. If a version has no date, it’s called out as NO DATE rather than treated as healthy.

The chain with no slack

The critical path is listed and every open issue is ranked by risk, so you know what to swarm first. Ask “what if this slips?” and the answer stays in memory. Slipstream never rewrites Jira.

The maths, in the open

Critical path

A forward and backward pass gives total and free float. The longest remaining chain sets the date.

PERT confidence

O = 0.8M · P = M × (1.5 + 0.25 × risk words)
E = (O + 4M + P) / 6

Variances add along the chain. Confidence is the normal probability of finishing by the Fix Version date.

FMEA risk (RPN)

RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detection

Each factor runs from 1 to 10. High is 150–249 and Critical is 250+. Severity reflects float and blast radius, Occurrence reflects carry-overs and staleness, and Detection reflects implicit links, missing owners and default estimates.

What-If

Delay an item or remove a dependency to see the new duration and which issues become critical. It runs on a copy, in memory, and never writes to Jira.

Stage-Gate call

Readiness starts at 100 and loses points for cycles, low confidence and high-RPN issues

HOLD
Any dependency cycle, or confidence below 50%.
NO DATE
No confidence, or an undated Fix Version that would otherwise be GO.
CONDITIONAL
Confidence from 50% to 80%, or any open high-RPN item.
GO
Confidence of 80% or more, no cycle, no open high-RPN item and no undated Fix Version.

The graph, path, confidence and RPN are all deterministic. A model only writes the short brief, and only on Paid and Evaluation. Read the full guide

Security and zero egress

A security review that fits on one page.

Slipstream runs entirely on Atlassian Forge. There’s no vendor server, webhook, analytics endpoint or third-party database, so your Jira data stays on Atlassian.

Read-only
It declares no write:jira-work and no Confluence scopes. It never posts Jira comments.
No repository access
There’s no /rest/dev-status call and no read:source-code scope.
Minimal personal data
The only person identifier stored is the assignee’s Atlassian account id.
30-day retention
Forge SQL rows older than 30 days are deleted daily. Every query is filtered by your installation’s cloud_id.
Model use stays on Atlassian
The brief is written by a model hosted by Atlassian through Forge LLM, on Paid and Evaluation only. Each scan sends at most 5 chains, with excerpts capped at 160 characters and sanitised. Free makes zero model calls. The model never computes the graph, path, confidence or RPN.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Can’t find what you need? Email support@criticalpathworks.com.

Does Slipstream read our pull requests or branches?+

No. Slipstream doesn’t connect to any repository and doesn’t call Jira’s development-information API. A branch, PR or commit name only counts when someone has already written it into a Jira comment or description.

Does it change anything in Jira?+

No. Access is read-only. Slipstream shows the gate and brief on its own page and in the issue panel, and doesn’t post Jira comments. What-If runs on an in-memory copy and never writes to Jira.

Does it send alerts to Slack, email or Confluence?+

Not in v1.0. There’s no Slack, email or webhook delivery and no Confluence publishing. There’s also no CSV import and no GitHub or other connector.

Does the app count seats?+

Free on the Atlassian Marketplace is for up to 10 users. Inside the app, Free is limited to 2 projects. An active Paid licence or Evaluation has no project cap and keeps the paid features at any seat count. Atlassian bills $4.00 per user per month, or $40 per user per year.

What happens when the Evaluation ends?+

Without a Paid licence, the site moves to Free: up to 10 Marketplace users, at most 2 projects inside the app, 1 scheduled and 1 manual scan per 24 hours, and a deterministic summary with no model calls.

How is this different from Jira Plans?+

Jira Plans comes with Jira Premium and Enterprise, which you buy for the whole site. Slipstream runs on your current Jira Cloud plan. It also adds implicit dependencies found in comments and descriptions, a PERT confidence for each Fix Version, an RPN for each open issue, and a Stage-Gate call.

How long is data kept?+

Rows older than 30 days are deleted daily from Forge SQL. The only person identifier stored is the assignee’s Atlassian account id.
